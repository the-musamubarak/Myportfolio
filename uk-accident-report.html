Section 01

Abstract

This report presents a structured statistical analysis of road traffic casualty data recorded across the United Kingdom during 2021 and 2022, sourced from a publicly available Kaggle dataset and visualised through a Power BI dashboard. The dataset captures a total of 418,000 individual casualties, classified by severity, associated with prevailing weather conditions at the time of the incident, distributed across the days of the week, and tracked chronologically across a 24-month accident date timeline. The objective of this report is to translate the dashboard's visual analytics into a structured narrative capable of informing road safety policy, enforcement resource allocation, and public safety communication strategy.

The headline findings reveal that fatal casualties, while numerically smaller than other severity categories at 7,135 incidents, represent the most consequential subset of the data from a public health and policy perspective, accounting for 1.71% of all recorded casualties. Serious casualties account for 14.19% of the total, while the overwhelming majority 84.1% are classified as slight. Weather analysis shows that fine conditions without high winds account for the largest share of casualties by a substantial margin, a finding that initially appears counterintuitive but is explained by exposure volume rather than per-journey risk. Day-of-week analysis identifies Friday as the peak casualty day, with a gradual decline through the week toward a Sunday minimum. The report concludes with policy-relevant recommendations targeting enforcement timing, weather-adjusted risk communication, and severity-specific intervention strategies.

Section 02

Introduction

Road traffic accidents remain one of the most persistent and quantifiable public safety challenges faced by modern transport systems. In the United Kingdom, the collection and publication of detailed road casualty statistics has long served as a foundational input to national road safety strategy, informing decisions ranging from speed limit policy and road design standards to seasonal public awareness campaigns and police enforcement deployment. The dataset examined in this report, drawn from official-style casualty records covering the 2021–2022 period, provides a granular view into the circumstances, severity, and temporal distribution of road traffic casualties across the country.

The period under examination is analytically significant for reasons beyond its intrinsic road safety value. 2021 and 2022 represent a transitional phase in the United Kingdom's post-pandemic recovery, during which traffic volumes, commuting patterns, and travel behaviour were in a state of flux as lockdown restrictions eased and working patterns shifted toward greater flexibility and hybrid arrangements. Road casualty data from this period therefore captures not only the underlying structural risk factors associated with road travel weather, time of week, road type, and speed environment but also the residual effects of a uniquely disrupted travel landscape.

This report was prepared as part of a data analytics portfolio project, using Microsoft Power BI as the primary tool for data transformation, modelling, and visualisation. The accompanying dashboard presents an interactive, filterable view of the casualty dataset, allowing users to segment the data by accident severity, road type, urban or rural classification, lighting conditions, and weather conditions. This written report draws directly from the dashboard's visual findings, providing narrative depth, contextual framing, and policy-oriented interpretation that complements the dashboard's interactive exploration capability.

It should be noted that road casualty statistics, by their nature, describe outcomes that are deeply consequential to the individuals and families involved. While this report adopts an analytical and statistical lens consistent with formal data reporting conventions, the underlying subject matter concerns real incidents of injury and loss of life. The analysis is conducted in the spirit of contributing to road safety understanding and prevention, and all interpretations should be read with appropriate sensitivity to the human dimension of the data.

Section 03

Background & Context

The United Kingdom has one of the most extensively documented road safety data ecosystems in the world. The Department for Transport (DfT) has published detailed road casualty statistics historically under the STATS19 reporting framework for several decades, providing researchers, policymakers, and road safety organisations with a consistent longitudinal dataset against which to measure the effectiveness of interventions. This data infrastructure has supported the UK's broader road safety strategy, which has pursued a long-term goal of reducing road deaths and serious injuries through a combination of engineering improvements, enforcement, vehicle safety regulation, and behavioural change campaigns.

The UK's road safety record, viewed in international comparative context, is generally regarded as strong. The country consistently ranks among the safer nations in Europe by fatalities per capita and per vehicle distance travelled, a position attributed to decades of sustained investment in road engineering, a mature vehicle safety inspection regime (the MOT test), and comparatively strict drink-driving and seatbelt enforcement relative to many international peers. Nevertheless, the absolute casualty figures remain substantial the 418,000 casualties recorded in this two-year dataset represent a continuing and significant public health burden, even against a backdrop of long-term improvement trends.

Within the casualty severity hierarchy, the 7,135 fatal casualties recorded in this dataset represent the irreducible human cost at the centre of all road safety analysis. While slight casualties vastly outnumber fatalities in raw count, fatal and serious casualties carry disproportionate weight in policy evaluation because they represent outcomes that road safety interventions are specifically designed to prevent.

The classification framework used in UK road casualty reporting Fatal, Serious, and Slight follows a standardised severity taxonomy that has remained broadly consistent across decades of data collection, enabling reliable year-on-year and period-on-period comparison. A casualty is classified based on clinical outcome assessment at the time of police reporting or subsequent medical confirmation, with Fatal referring to death within 30 days of the collision as a result of the incident, Serious referring to injuries requiring hospital admission or involving conditions such as fractures or severe lacerations, and Slight covering minor injuries such as bruising or shock not requiring hospital admission.

The 2021–2022 period also coincides with notable shifts in UK transport policy discourse, including expanded investment in active travel infrastructure (cycling and walking), the rollout of additional 20mph speed limit zones in urban areas across parts of Wales and various English local authorities, and continued debate over the role of average speed cameras and automated enforcement technology. These policy developments form an important backdrop against which the casualty patterns identified in this dataset should be interpreted.

Section 04

Data & Methodology

The dataset analysed in this report is the publicly available UK Road Accident dataset covering the 2021–2022 period, sourced from Kaggle and structured around individual casualty-level records. Each record is associated with a range of attributes including accident date, accident severity classification (Fatal, Serious, Slight), road type, urban or rural area designation, prevailing light conditions at the time of the incident, weather conditions, and posted speed limit. The dataset was imported into Microsoft Power BI Desktop, where it was modelled, transformed, and visualised using a series of interactive charts and key performance indicator cards.

The dashboard design follows Power BI's standard dark theme, employing a charcoal-navy background palette with teal as the primary data visualisation accent colour a neutral, high-contrast scheme well suited to the serious and policy-relevant nature of road safety data. Five interactive filter controls are provided across the top of the dashboard, enabling users to slice the data by Year, Accident_Severity, Road_Type, Urban_or_Rural_Area, and Light_Conditions, alongside Weather_Conditions. This filtering architecture allows the dashboard to support both high-level overview analysis and granular investigation of specific casualty subsets for example, isolating fatal casualties occurring on rural roads during darkness, a combination of factors commonly associated with elevated risk in road safety literature.

Core KPI measures were calculated using DAX formulas within Power BI, including Total Casualties, Fatal Casualties, Serious Casualties, Slight Casualties, and Average Speed Limit. The temporal trend chart employs a continuous date-axis line visualisation, plotting total casualties against accident date across the full 24-month period from January 2021 through to the end of 2022. The severity breakdown is visualised through a donut chart displaying percentage shares, while weather conditions and day-of-week patterns are presented through horizontal and vertical bar charts respectively, ranked by casualty volume.

As with any large-scale administrative or crowd-sourced casualty dataset, several methodological caveats apply. Road casualty data is dependent on police-reported incidents, and reporting completeness can vary by severity category fatal and serious casualties are reported with near-complete consistency due to mandatory medical and police involvement, whereas minor slight-injury incidents may be subject to some degree of underreporting, particularly where injuries are minor and no police attendance is requested. The weather and light condition fields reflect the prevailing conditions recorded at the scene and should be interpreted as contextual factors rather than definitively established causal contributors to each incident.

Section 05

Casualty Severity Analysis

The severity distribution presented in the dashboard's donut chart is the single most consequential analytical lens applied to this dataset. Of the 418,000 total casualties recorded across 2021 and 2022, 84.1% are classified as Slight, 14.19% as Serious, and 1.71% as Fatal. In absolute terms, this translates to approximately 351,000 slight casualties, 59,000 serious casualties, and 7,135 fatal casualties. This distribution follows a pattern that is broadly consistent with established road safety statistical norms internationally the overwhelming majority of road traffic incidents result in outcomes that, while disruptive and often painful for those involved, do not result in life-threatening injury.

However, the analytical and policy significance of casualty data cannot be assessed by raw proportion alone. The 7,135 fatal casualties recorded in this dataset, while representing less than two percent of the total casualty count, constitute the most severe and irreversible category of outcome that road safety policy seeks to prevent. Each fatal casualty represents the loss of a human life, with consequences extending far beyond the statistical record to affect families, communities, and the broader social fabric. Road safety researchers and policymakers therefore typically apply severity-weighted analytical frameworks sometimes formalised through metrics such as Killed or Seriously Injured (KSI) counts that elevate the analytical priority of fatal and serious outcomes above their raw proportional share of the dataset.

A casualty severity distribution skewed heavily toward slight injuries is not, in itself, evidence of acceptable road safety performance. It reflects the underlying physics of most low-speed collisions, but the persistent presence of over 7,000 fatal casualties and nearly 59,000 serious casualties across just two years signals that meaningful opportunities for further harm reduction remain.

The combined Fatal and Serious casualty count sometimes referred to in UK road safety terminology as KSI (Killed or Seriously Injured) totals approximately 66,135 across the two-year period, representing 15.9% of all casualties. This KSI metric is frequently used by UK local authorities and national transport bodies as the primary performance indicator against which road safety strategy targets are set, given its closer correlation with the genuine human and economic cost of road traffic collisions compared to the total casualty figure alone.

Section 11

Conclusion

This report has presented a comprehensive statistical analysis of UK road traffic casualty data spanning the 2021–2022 period, drawing on a dataset of 418,000 recorded casualties visualised through an interactive Power BI dashboard. Through structured examination of casualty severity, temporal trends, and day-of-week patterns, this analysis has surfaced findings of direct relevance to road safety policy and enforcement planning.

The severity analysis confirms that while the majority of casualties are classified as slight, the continuing presence of over 7,000 fatal and nearly 59,000 serious casualties across just two years represents a substantial ongoing public safety challenge that merits sustained and severity-weighted policy attention. The persistent identification of temporal and geographic patterns demonstrates the value of data-driven road safety strategy.

Ultimately, behind every statistic presented in this report lies a real human experience a collision, an injury, in the most serious cases a life lost. The purpose of rigorous data analysis in this domain is action: translating patterns in the data into targeted, evidence-based interventions that can meaningfully reduce the human cost of road travel in the years ahead.
