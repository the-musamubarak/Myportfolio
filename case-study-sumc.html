From paper ledgers to peace of mind: an offline inventory system for a Kaduna security doors business

The client

A busy shop with high-value stock.

Alhaji Usman's shop in Panteka, Kaduna sells security doors and wallpaper. Doors are not small purchases: in the system's catalogue, prices run from ₦58,000 for a steel door up to ₦250,000 for a double door. Every unit that is miscounted, mispriced or sold on credit and forgotten is real money.

Until now the business ran on manual paper records for stock, sales and what customers owed. Paper depends on memory and on every entry being written correctly, and the more products, customers and staff are involved, the harder that becomes to guarantee.

I set the design goals from the start: a tool the team can learn in a day, that works without internet or a monthly subscription, and that lets the owner see the whole business on one screen instead of piecing it together from paper.

Design goals

  • Know exactly what is in stock
  • Stop losing track of who owes money
  • See profit, not just sales
  • Keep staff accountable
  • Work with no internet
  • Simple enough for everyday use

The problem with paper

Paper records fail quietly, and the owner pays for it.

Paper is cheap and familiar, which is exactly why businesses stay with it long after it has stopped working. The trouble is that its failures are silent. A wrong total, a missing line or an unrecorded debt does not announce itself. It shows up weeks later as money that cannot be explained.

Stock you cannot trust

To know how many of a particular door are left, someone has to count or add up pages of entries. Deliveries and sales written late or not at all mean the book and the shelf slowly stop matching.

Arithmetic errors

Totals worked out by hand on every sale invite small mistakes. On items costing tens or hundreds of thousands of naira, one slip can cost more than a day's profit.

Debts that live in memory

Credit sales are the lifeblood of building supplies and also the easiest money to lose. A debt book makes it hard to see the total owed, how old each balance is, or what has been part-paid.

Profit is a guess

Sales are easy to see on paper. Profit needs the cost of every item compared with its selling price. Few paper systems do this, so the owner knows how much came in but not how much he actually made.

No accountability

Anyone can write in a book, in any handwriting, and entries can be changed. The cost price of every product is visible to whoever holds the book, and it is hard to tell which staff member made which sale.

Slow, unreliable reports

A monthly summary means hours of adding up. Best sellers, slow movers and trends stay hidden, so restocking is done by habit instead of by evidence.

One fragile book

A single physical book can be damaged by water, lost, misplaced or altered. If it goes missing, so does the memory of the business.

Constant background worry

The hardest cost is not on any page: the owner cannot step away without wondering what is being written, what is being missed and what is being forgotten.

The solution

Everything the business did on paper, in one place.

I designed and built a desktop application that installs on the shop's own Windows computer and keeps all of its data in a local database. There is no subscription, no cloud account to manage and no internet needed to make a sale. The screens follow the way the shop actually works: record a sale, receive stock, check what a customer owes, read the day's numbers.

Live dashboard

Sales, receivables, low-stock items, stock value and estimated profit or loss for the day on one screen.

Products with photos

A visual catalogue with price, cost (admin only), category and stock for every door, searchable by name, brand or SKU.

Fast sales entry

Pick a product, set the price, record the amount paid. The system works out the balance due and updates stock instantly.

Categories per product line

Each category collects the right details for that type of product, and new lines such as wallpaper can be added.

Supplier purchases

Stock arriving from suppliers is recorded and added to inventory automatically, with a full history.

Customers & debtors

See who owes what, how much has been paid, and mark balances paid as customers settle them.

Returns & receipts

Returns are recorded with reasons and reflected in net revenue and profit, and sales keep a receipt trail.

Reports & exports

Any date range, daily or monthly trends, revenue by category, profit by product and receivables aging, exportable to CSV.

Owner & staff roles

The owner controls accounts. Staff can sell and look up stock without seeing cost prices.

A day with the system

How the daily routine changed.

Open the shop, open the dashboard

Before the first customer arrives, the owner sees yesterday's activity, what is outstanding and which doors are running low. No books are opened.

Make a sale

Staff search the product, add it to the cart, choose the customer and payment method and enter the amount paid. The total and any balance are calculated and stock reduces automatically.

Sell on credit without losing track

If the customer pays part now, the rest is recorded as an unpaid balance against their name, ready to be collected and marked paid later.

Receive new stock

When a delivery arrives, it is recorded as a purchase from the supplier and the quantities go straight into inventory.

Close the day with certainty

The dashboard shows the day's sales, estimated profit and sales by staff member. The owner knows how the day went in seconds, not after an evening of adding up.

Plan the week with real numbers

Reports show best sellers, profit by product and ageing debts, so reordering and collections are based on evidence.

What it gives the owner: peace of mind

Features matter, but the real change is how it feels to own the business.

I know what is in my shop

Stock is a number on the screen that moves with every sale and delivery, not a figure someone remembers or a count that takes a day.

I know who owes me

Every unpaid balance has a name, an amount and a date. Money owed stops being a worry at the back of the mind.

I know what I really made

Estimated profit and loss are shown for the day, and profit by product shows which doors actually earn their place on the shelf.

I know what my staff are doing

Each sale is linked to the person who made it. Staff sign in with their own accounts and do not see cost prices.

No internet, no problem

The system runs on the shop's own computer. No network, no data bundle, no waiting for a website to load and no sale lost to a bad connection.

My records belong to me

There is no cloud subscription and no outside company holding the shop's numbers. The data stays in the shop.

The goal was simple: the owner can leave the counter and still know exactly how the business is doing.

Design decisions that mattered

Built around the realities of a small business in Nigeria.

1

Offline first

Unreliable internet is part of everyday life, so the app never depends on it. Everything is stored in a local database on the shop's computer.

2

Roles for owner and staff

The first account created becomes the admin. The admin adds staff accounts and controls who can administer the store. Sensitive information such as cost prices is hidden from staff, and checks are enforced in the application's back end, not just hidden on screen.

3

Secure sign-in

Passwords are stored only as hashes and never as readable text. The owner can deactivate an account instantly.

4

Stock that updates itself

Sales reduce stock and purchases increase it automatically, so the numbers on screen match the shelf without anyone counting.

5

Flexible categories

Doors, wallpaper and any future product line need different details. Categories define what is collected for each, so data stays consistent without forcing every product into one form.

6

Simple enough to learn in a day

A clear sidebar, large buttons and plain language follow the shop's routine, so staff do not need training in software.
