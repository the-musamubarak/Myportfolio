Netflix Content Analysis

Data source: Netflix Titles Dataset | Tool: Microsoft Power BI | © 2026 Musa Mubarak Abdulaziz

Executive Summary

This report presents a structured analysis of Netflix's global content catalogue, comprising 8,807 titles spanning movies and TV shows released between 1920 and 2021. The analysis explores content distribution by type, rating classification, and geographic origin, offering data-driven insights into Netflix's content strategy and audience targeting approach.

1. Content Type Distribution

The Netflix catalogue is predominantly movie-driven, with 6,131 movies accounting for 69.62% of all titles, compared to 2,676 TV shows representing the remaining 30.38%. This 7:3 ratio indicates that Netflix built its library foundation on film licensing before scaling into original and acquired television content. The presence of both content types reflects a dual strategy aimed at serving both casual viewers seeking standalone films and subscribers who prefer long-form episodic storytelling.

2. Content Growth Over Time

The timeline analysis reveals a near-flat content addition rate from the 1920s through the 1990s, consistent with the platform's early reliance on legacy film libraries. A dramatic acceleration begins around 2010, coinciding with Netflix's transition from a DVD rental service to a global streaming platform. Content additions peaked sharply between 2018 and 2019, reflecting the height of Netflix's aggressive catalogue expansion strategy ahead of intensifying competition from Disney+, HBO Max, and Amazon Prime Video. The visible decline post-2020 suggests a strategic pivot from volume to quality, with greater investment in original productions rather than third-party acquisitions.

3. Content Rating Analysis

TV-MA (Mature Audiences) dominates the catalogue with over 3,200 titles, followed by TV-14 at approximately 2,160 titles. Together, these two adult-oriented ratings account for roughly 60% of all content. TV-PG, R, and PG-13 represent a secondary tier serving teen and general audiences. Children's ratings such as TV-Y, TV-Y7, and TV-G make up a comparatively small share, suggesting Netflix's primary subscriber demographic skews toward adults aged 18 and above. This rating distribution also aligns with Netflix's global branding as a platform that does not shy away from mature, complex, and socially challenging narratives.

4. Geographic Content Distribution

The world map visual confirms the United States as the dominant content-producing country by a significant margin, reflecting Hollywood's historical role as the world's largest film and television industry. However, notable content volumes from the United Kingdom, India, Canada, and parts of Europe and Asia signal Netflix's deliberate investment in regional and international content. This geographic diversification is consistent with Netflix's localisation strategy, which produced hits such as Money Heist (Spain), Sacred Games (India), and Dark (Germany), helping the platform grow its subscriber base across non-English-speaking markets.

5. Average Duration Insight

The dashboard surfaces three duration benchmarks: 66 minutes, 74 minutes, and 84 minutes, representing the range of average movie runtimes across the catalogue. This suggests Netflix's film library spans short-form content (documentaries, animated features) through standard theatrical releases, catering to varying viewer attention spans and consumption habits.

Conclusion

Netflix's content catalogue reflects a platform that has matured from a film aggregator into a sophisticated global media network. The data points to a clear adult-first content philosophy, a strategic peak-and-retreat pattern in catalogue expansion, and a growing commitment to international content diversity. For any data analyst or business intelligence professional, this dataset serves as a compelling case study in how streaming platforms use content strategy as a competitive weapon.

Spotify Streaming Analysis

Data source: Spotify Listening Data | Tool: Microsoft Power BI | © 2026 Musa Mubarak Abdulaziz

Executive Summary

This report presents a comprehensive analysis of personal Spotify listening data, capturing music preferences and streaming patterns across a curated collection of 25 artists and contemporary tracks. The dataset spans April through July 2024, offering insights into artist distribution, genre preferences, and popularity metrics within the streaming ecosystem.

1. Artist Distribution & Diversity

The analysed dataset comprises 25 distinct artists, with prominent representation from Latin music genres including reggaeton, Latin pop, and Latin trap. Leading artists such as Myke Towers, Bad Bunny, and Shakira dominate the catalogue, reflecting contemporary streaming trends toward Latin music on global platforms. The distribution reveals both established legacy artists and emerging contemporary performers, indicating a balanced exposure to diverse musical voices.

2. Streaming Patterns & Release Timing

The temporal analysis shows a notable concentration of high-engagement content released in May 2024, with 19 tracks recorded during this single month compared to 2 in April and 3 in July. This pattern suggests either a targeted listening period or a release cluster event that drove significant platform activity during the analysed timeframe.

3. Popularity & Engagement Metrics

The dataset achieves a mean popularity score of 87 out of 100 — placing it firmly in the upper tier of global Spotify content. This elevated average indicates exposure to highly-streamed, algorithmically favoured releases. For context, the median track on Spotify scores below 50, making this dataset's performance significantly above typical platform benchmarks.

4. Track Duration & Production Characteristics

Tracks in the dataset maintain a consistent average duration of 4.64 minutes, aligning with industry-standard radio-ready lengths optimized for streaming platforms. The uniform duration profile suggests tracks were engineered for maximum discoverability and algorithmic placement, reflecting contemporary music production practices designed to enhance completion rates and platform engagement.

5. Content Classification & Explicit Material

The dataset contains 25 explicit-rated tracks, representing mature content across hip-hop, R&B, and Latin music genres. This distribution aligns with streaming platform trends showing that adult-oriented content commands significant engagement and listener attention within contemporary music consumption patterns.

Conclusion

This Spotify analysis reveals a listener profile deeply engaged with high-performance contemporary music, particularly within Latin, R&B, and hip-hop genres. The elevated popularity scores, concentrated release timing, and artist composition suggest a curated exposure to algorithmic winners and trending content on the streaming platform. The data demonstrates how individual listening behaviour reflects broader platform trends and the strategic release patterns employed by contemporary artists to maximize engagement and discoverability.
